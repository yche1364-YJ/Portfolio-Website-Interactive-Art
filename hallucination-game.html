01 / THE CONCEPT

One city. Two ways of seeing.

Lost in Light is a proposed browser-based puzzle adventure set in a mysterious city built from RGB neon lines. The player is an invisible light guide, helping a fictional glowing creature reach its destination.

The creature cannot perceive red light, but can perceive green and blue. Players freely draw light, combine colors, and eventually redirect beams with mirrors to make unclear routes visible. Switching perspectives reveals whether those changes actually help the creature navigate.

The hallucination theme comes from the gap between perception and reality: optical illusions, RGB misregistration, overlapping light, and misleading paths. The creature’s vision is fictional; the proposal does not equate color-vision differences with clinical hallucinations.

02 / CASE STUDY

What drawing-based puzzles taught me

I explored Hue by TheHueCreators ↗, a free browser game distinct from Hue (2016) by Fiddlesticks. In my playthrough, limited ink, disappearing lines, and slow character movement made drawing a problem of timing as well as placement.

  1. Resources shape decisions

    Limited ink and temporary lines made the amount and timing of each drawing matter.

  2. Drawing depends on movement

    A useful platform had to match the character’s speed, jumping distance, and route.

  3. Failure invites revision

    Returning to the start encouraged me to rethink the drawing rather than repeat the same action.

My takeaway: keep the freedom of drawing, but change its purpose. Instead of creating platforms, the player adds light. Environmental clues and the creature stopping should explain where—and why—to intervene.

03 / CRITICAL DISCUSSION WITH AI

From fixed answers to exploration

AI discussions helped me explore RGB mixing and optical illusions, but two suggestions needed a clearer design position.

Drawing freedom vs. guidance

The initial suggestion was to restrict drawing to fixed interaction points. I felt that would turn exploration into filling in predetermined answers.

-> Decision: allow free drawing. Use unclear paths, creature behavior, and mirrors as clues instead of prescribing a drawing location.

Color perception vs. hallucination

Removing a color alone did not explain the hallucination theme.

-> Decision: create a fictional perceptual system, with overlapping light, misregistered RGB edges, and false paths that ask players to compare perspectives.

04 / INTERACTION

Observe. Draw. Switch. Navigate.

Neon Marker
Draw red, green, and blue light freely with the mouse.
RGB mixing
Combine channels to change path visibility: red + green → yellow; green + blue → cyan; red + blue → magenta; all three → white.
Mirror reflection
Redirect light toward another area. This is a stretch goal for the first prototype.
Creature Vision
Use the bottom-left creature icon to enter two overlapping vision circles; move the mouse to look around.
Automatic navigation
The creature attempts to follow a visible path. When the path becomes unreadable, it stops so the player can return and revise the light.

05 / PLAYER JOURNEY

A path from uncertainty to understanding

Opening

Discover the difference

Explore the city and learn that the creature cannot see red paths.

Stage 1

Reveal an invisible road

Add green or blue light so the creature can recognize the route.

Stage 2

Redirect the light

Combine colors and use mirrors to illuminate another section.

Stage 3

Question a false path

Compare views and correct misleading light patterns.

Ending

Reach the exit

The creature arrives and the city lights become stable.

This is the longer-term experience. The initial build focuses on one playable puzzle.

06 / STYLE & SOUND

Neon line art × RGB misregistration

Visual direction

A dark, top-down city with geometric neon outlines, a glowing creature, soft flicker, and subtle channel offsets. Space Grotesk or Inter would keep the game interface clear.

References include TRON for glowing geometry, Mini Metro for route clarity, and Hue (2016) for color-based environmental interaction.

Planned audio

Minimal electronic ambience and soft synthesized feedback for drawing, mixing light, switching vision, and solving a puzzle. Mysterious and digital, without becoming frightening.

I plan to generate audio with ElevenLabs. Final assets, prompts, and credits will be documented after production.

07 / BUILD PLAN

One playable puzzle first

Week 2 · Core prototype

Build the interaction loop

Create one neon-city area with RGB drawing, color mixing, two-circle Creature Vision, and automatic creature movement.

Week 3 · Test & refine

Make the rules understandable

Test perception, drawing freedom, and environmental guidance. Refine contrast, camera movement, feedback, and audio. Add basic mirror reflection if time allows.

Expected outcome: a playable HTML5 prototype demonstrating light drawing and perspective switching. The full three-stage journey is planned for later development.
